# Mars-Rover
INTRO
Hello everyone! I hope you guys are having a wonderful day! This project is about a 3D Miniature Mars Rover made on Onshape which took about 24 hours to make. I got this idea because I was like, “Stardance is about space, so I’ll just make a rover!”

What Is It?
	It is a Mars rover, made to be small and basically a mini version of a rover. Why is it mini? This is because regular sized rovers are about 10 feet long, this rover is about 3 feet. Big rovers cost lots of money and effort and time because they are huge and cannot be mass produced, as well as the amount of effort to make sure this 10 foot gargantuan lands properly. The rover I produced can be mass produced and a lot of them can be sent to Mars in one ship compared to big rovers. Even though it is small, it still has all the necessary components that a rover should have, which I will go into later in this document. This rover will primarily be made out of metals so it weighs more and has a more stable center. Keep in mind, Mars has roughly a third of Earth’s gravity, so it needs to be heavy.

What does it contain?
	This rover has a lot of different attributes. Let's start with the structure that holds up the rover and helps it roll through the Martian environment. This structure is called a Rocker- Bogie system. It consists of 2 parts of the wheel framework that attach to each other and rotate. The two parts allow the whole vehicle to be stable, which is extremely important on mars. This rotation allows the rover to go over several bumps or tiny high hills without crashing. For your reference, here's a picture of the system that I made.
	Next, the wheels. Nothing very complicated, just some heavy duty wheels that I imported and scaled so it would fit my robot. They rotate and have grooves for better grip, made with plastic and metal. If you turn my robot around, on the back it has cameras that allows the vehicle to see the back. On top of the body, at the very top there is a Pancam Mast Assembly (PMA) which is a tall elevated mast used on my rover to hold panoramic and navigation cameras, which is also seen on the Mars rovers Spirit and Opportunity. The top also contains a solar panel so these mini ‘lil bots can get the solar energy to work. I decided against the radioactive plutonium used for other Rovers by using the natural decay as a power source and source for heat, also it’d be expensive and difficult to use. Next it has a low gain antenna that looks like a stick. It serves as a backup or failsafe to receive direct-from-Earth commands without needing to precisely point, unlike the high gain antenna that needs to point to get a signal. The high gain antenna looks like a broken pickleball paddle and concentrates its radioactive waves in a concentrated beam.
	Next, let's move to the inside of the rover’s body. At the front of the rover’s body there is a pair of a hinge door that can open and close to protect the inside. Then, behind that, we see two rods and  2 mechanical arms in which each mechanical claw is made up of 3 parts, where there is 2 long strips of metal that connect to each other and a claw that can go big or small, and the other arm has a drillbit, used to interact and break martian materials for sample storing. Each arm stands on a rod, allowing them to change direction vertically making the process of extracting materials very easy. Next there are two solar panels that are attached to a cylindrical piston that expands and retracts to push the solar panels out then pull them back inside, so the solar panels can be put away safely at night. Finally at the back, 4 Mars Ascent Vehicles that are stored so the robot can store samples inside and allow these rockets to eject and safely travel back to Earth for more experiments.
	So, that’s it! My Mini Mars rover broken down! Be careful while playing around with the live link, as parts can glitch because of the sheer amount of parts inside my rover! Also be aware, a lot of the mates (mates are basically bolts and different types of screws that allow movement, for example spin,not move, or spin in all 3 axes!)
